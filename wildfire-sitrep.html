Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

Elevated to locally critical conditions persist across the northern half of the West today. Hot, unstable conditions with 3-15% RH and poor overnight recovery will lead to extended burn periods. Breezy westerly winds will gust between 25 and 40 mph across much of the Northwest; strongest in the Cascade Gaps and along the northern Sierra Front. While cooler air is filtering west of the Cascades and lowering temperatures slightly in the Upper Columbia Basin, maximum temperatures will still exceed 100°F in lower elevations across California, the Great Basin, the central Rockies, the central and southern Plains, and the Southwest. Wet thunderstorm coverage remains well below average in the Southwest, with activity focused along the Mogollon Rim and southeast Arizona. However, there is concern for isolated dry lightning this afternoon extending into the central Sierra, southern Nevada, southern Utah, northern Arizona, and parts of central and northern New Mexico. Elsewhere, the eastern U.S. will see near-normal temperatures, while increased cloud cover and scattered thunderstorms bring slightly below-normal temperatures to the Lower Mississippi Valley and Gulf Coast. Warm, dry conditions continue in eastern Alaska, maintaining elevated potential in the Upper Tanana Valley. Additionally, critically dry fuels combined with hot, dry, and breezy conditions will result in high-risk significant fire potential in Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 0102 AM CDT Sat Aug 08 2026  ·  Valid 081200Z - 091200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

Most of the region will see the development of surface winds around 15 mph with higher gusts as diurnal heating and the associated deep vertical mixing bring down modest mid-level flow. The deep mixing will also contribute to relative humidity falling to perhaps as low as 5% in favored areas across the region, with minimum afternoon relative humidity between 10-15% elsewhere. Given very receptive fuels, elevated to locally critical fire weather conditions are likely.

Similar to Friday, thunderstorms are expected along the western periphery of a mid-level ridge where the advancing mid- and upper-level monsoonal moisture will be found. Beneath this moist plume, a very dry sub-cloud layer will support significant evaporation of falling precipitation leading to little, if any, appreciable rainfall. Evaporative cooling beneath these dry thunderstorms will result in gusty/erratic thunderstorm outflows.

Model guidance is in good agreement with a belt of modest southwesterly 700-millibar flow stretching from southeast Colorado toward the Great Lakes. This flow should easily mix down to the surface during the afternoon as strong diurnal heating mixes into and through this layer. The result will be gusty surface winds between 15-20 in the presence of minimum relative humidity falling into the teens. Seasonally adjusted ERC percentiles show a very dry fuelscape, with percentiles between 95-98%. A small elevated area was introduced to cover this potential.
