Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

Breezy westerly winds of 15–25 mph, with gusts of 25–35 mph, are expected across much of the Intermountain West and northern Rockies. Gusts up to 45–50 mph are possible across parts of the northern Rockies, southern Nevada, Utah, northwest Colorado, and Wyoming. RH will generally range from 5–25%, with locally higher values across Nevada and northern Utah. RH of 10–20% will persist across inland California and the northern Great Basin, while 12–20% RH is expected from north Texas into Oklahoma. Isolated to scattered mixed wet/dry thunderstorms are expected across the central and eastern Great Basin into the central Rockies, particularly from eastern Nevada and southwest Utah into northwest Colorado and southern Wyoming. Scattered mixed wet/dry thunderstorms are also expected across the Southwest, primarily in Arizona and west of the Divide in New Mexico. Scattered thunderstorms, some potentially strong to severe, are forecast along and ahead of a cold front from the northern Plains into the Upper Midwest. Isolated to scattered thunderstorms are expected across Florida and the northern Gulf Coast, while most of the eastern U.S. remains dry with near- to slightly-below- normal temperatures.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 1023 AM CDT Sat Aug 29 2026  ·  Valid 291700Z - 301200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

No changes were made to the Elevated wind/RH area in place over portions of the Pacific Northwest and adjoining regions as well the area over the southern Plains. The latest forecast guidance remains on track with previous outlook issuances. Surface observations as of late this morning indicate south-southwesterly winds of 15-20 mph in place over the southern Plains as the surface trough tightens the pressure gradient. A well-mixed boundary layer amid hot surface temperatures will support afternoon RHs below 20-25% across the highlighted area.

Sufficient instability (CAPE near 100-500 J/kg) will support wet and dry thunderstorms over portions of north-central WA later this afternoon amid increasing dynamic lift within the left exit region of the jet streak ahead of the approaching trough. While moisture content ahead of this trough remains low (precipitable water values near 0.5-0.6"), much below normal temperatures today and accumulating rainfall likely on Day 2/Sunday where convection is most likely to occur this afternoon. Continued cooler than normal conditions and higher RHs persist through much of the coming week over this area as well. While a few new lightning ignitions are not out of the question due to dry (but moistening) fuels, they will be significantly moderated by the aforementioned environmental conditions over the next several days.
