Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

Lightning activity is expected to trend downward across much of the West as compared to Monday with mainly isolated mixed wet/dry thunderstorms favoring southwest Montana into central Idaho and northwest Wyoming, as well as the eastern Great Basin into the Southwest. It will also be warmer-than-normal in the West, and extreme heat will dominate in southeast California, central/southern Arizona and parts of New Mexico where lower-elevation highs will approach record values of 100°F to 115°F as the monsoon wanes. Expect locally breezy westerly winds along the eastern slopes of the southern Cascades into southeast Oregon and the Sierra Front with generally light winds elsewhere in the West. A slow-moving moist front backing in from the east will boost wetting thunderstorm coverage for Colorado (east of the Divide) and northeast New Mexico. Ahead of the cold front, abnormally hot conditions, gusty southerly winds, and single-digit to low-double-digit RH persists from Texas into southern Oklahoma and Arkansas. Isolated thunderstorms will be found in the Lower Mississippi Valley with better wetting rain chances in southern Georgia and Florida. Alaska's eastern Interior stays cool and showery, as does much of the Ohio Valley and Northeast, while trade winds weaken in Hawai'i under more stable conditions.

...RED FLAG WARNING IN EFFECT 10 AM TUESDAY TO 3 PM FRIDAY FOR MANY INTERIOR PORTIONS OF SOUTHWEST CALIFORNIA DUE TO EXTREME HEAT AND INSTABILITY ALONG WITH LOCALLY GUSTY WINDS AND LOW HUMIDITIES... .A very hot and unstable air mass combined with locally gusty winds and low humidities will bring a significant risk of large plume dominated fires with rapid spread and extreme fire behavior. The National Weather Service in Los Angeles/Oxnard has issued a Red Flag Warning for extreme heat and instability along with locally gusty winds and low humidities, which is in effect from 10 AM Tuesday to 3 PM PDT Friday. * TEMPERATURES...Highs 96 to 106 across the lower elevations. Highs 85 to 95 common across the higher mountains. Lows in the mid 70s to mid 80s common in the mountains and foothills. * INSTABILITY...Very deep mixing heights of 12,000 to 18,000 feet Tuesday through Thursday, slightly lower on Friday. * WINDS...Southwest 10 to 20 mph with gusts 20 to 30 mph each afternoon into early evening. * RELATIVE HUMIDITY...Minimum humidities 10 to 20 percent each day, Poor recoveries overnight hours in the mountains and foothills with humidities in the 15 to 30 percent range. * IMPACTS...There is a significant risk of large plume dominated fires with rapid fire spread and extreme fire behavior. There is a history of large plume dominated fires this time of year with similar weather and fuel conditions, including the Bridge, Bobcat, and Station fires. PRECAUTIONARY/PREPAREDNESS ACTIONS... A Red Flag Warning means that critical fire weather conditions will occur shortly. Use extreme caution with anything that can spark a wildfire. Residents near wildland interfaces should be prepared to evacuate if a wildfire breaks out. See readyforwildfire.org and wildfirerisk.org for information.
