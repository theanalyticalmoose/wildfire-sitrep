Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

8/4/2026 Cooler-than-average temperatures are expected for the northern and central Plains, northern Rockies, and western Great Lakes, while remaining slightly below normal east of the Mississippi River. Extreme heat will persist throughout inland California, the central/southern Great Basin, and the Southwest into the southern Plains, with highs reaching 100°F or above. Temperatures will be near or slightly above normal for the Inland Northwest. Hot, dry, and unstable conditions will expand over western portions of the Northwest. A strengthening thermal trough creates high-risk fire potential for southwest Oregon, extending north along the Cascades to the Columbia River Gorge. Lingering breeziness amidst a very hot, dry, and unstable air mass creates similar high- risk fire conditions for the northern and eastern Great Basin. Minimum RH of 3-15% across California, the Inland Northwest, the Great Basin, and the Four Corners, and 10-20% in northern Idaho and Montana. Isolated mixed wet/dry thunderstorms from the Rockies into the northern Plains. A stronger signal for wet thunderstorms exists in southeast Arizona and southwest New Mexico, though the rest of the Southwest will see below average coverage. Showers and thunderstorms will also favor the Carolinas, Georgia, and Florida.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 1235 AM CDT Tue Aug 04 2026  ·  Valid 041200Z - 051200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

A weak surface low will emerge across the Four Corners region, tightening a surface pressure gradient along the West Slope; as a result, gusty winds are expected across portions of the Great Basin and Colorado Plateau this afternoon. Strong daytime mixing will result in RH values of 5-15% and sustained westerly winds of 12-20 mph (gusts up to 30 mph) amid receptive fuels, maintaining Elevated fire weather highlights. Farther west, hot, dry, and unstable conditions are expected along/near a thermal trough extending out of northern California into western Oregon/Washington. Lighter winds across the spatial region preclude an introduction of fire weather highlights; however, gusty winds in terrain-favored areas could result in locally elevated conditions.
