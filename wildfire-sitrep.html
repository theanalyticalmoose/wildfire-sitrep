Predictive Services Discussion

Isolated dry thunderstorms may be ongoing this morning across southern Idaho, with isolated to scattered mixed wet/dry thunderstorms expected this afternoon from Utah into much of Wyoming and portions of Montana with some new ignitions possible. No thunderstorms are expected in the Northwest amid minimum RH of 10-20% except for a very slight chance of a thunderstorm in the Blue Mountains. Isolated to scattered wet monsoon thunderstorms continue in the Southwest and Colorado. Temperatures near to slightly below normal will be common in the Northwest, while hot temperatures (85-100°F) are expected across eastern Montana northeast Wyoming, the Dakotas to Minnesota while very hot temperatures (95-110°F) continue in the southern Plains. Slight cooling is expected in the Lower Mississippi Valley due to a weak cold front that will result in isolated thunderstorms and possibly a few new ignitions. Temperatures will trend 5-15°F above normal Southwest as the monsoon weakens, with similar above normal temperatures in most of California and the Great Basin and low afternoon RH. Farther east, cooler conditions along much of the East Coast. Scattered thunderstorms in South Georgia and Florida which will continue to bring temporary relief. High-risk significant fire potential for burn environment in portions of north Texas and western Oklahoma amid persistently very hot temperatures. Increased winds are expected near the Hawai’i as Tropical Storm Fausto passes to the north of the islands.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 1234 AM CDT Wed Jul 29 2026  ·  Valid 291200Z - 301200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

Locally elevated conditions are likely to develop across portions of Nevada with forecast southerly to westerly sustained winds of 10-15 mph with gusts of 20-25 mph amid minimum RH of 5-15%. The increase of breezy winds will be along a surface thermal trough and below slightly increased winds aloft due to a passing weak mid-level shortwave trough. Active large fires near the periphery of monsoonal moisture and thunderstorm chances in the central/eastern Great Basin may produce deep pyroconvection as well.
