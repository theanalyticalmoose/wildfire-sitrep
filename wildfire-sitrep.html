Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

Widespread critical conditions will develop across the Inland Northwest into the northern Rockies and northwestern Great Basin today from westerly winds 15-30 mph with gusts 30-45 mph amid relative humidity of 5-15%. Elevated conditions from slightly weaker westerly winds are expected in central Idaho to southwest and central Montana, as well, where temperatures will be up to 20°F above normal. Very hot, record-setting temperatures 100-110°F are expected in southeast Montana into the western Dakotas with relative humidity below 20%, and isolated to scattered mixed wet and dry thunderstorms will develop resulting in several new ignitions. Hot and dry conditions are expected in much of central and southern California, while isolated mixed wet and dry thunderstorms develop in central Nevada to southeast Idaho and northern Utah. Isolated wet monsoon thunderstorms are expected in the Southwest into the central Rockies. Farther east, breezy conditions and low relative humidity and hot temperatures will support elevated fire weather conditions across the southern High Plains. In Minnesota, breezy northwest wind are expected amid hot temperatures and relative humidity of 25-40%. Isolated to scattered showers and thunderstorms will develop in the Great Lakes and Southeast, with scattered thunderstorms in Florida capable of new ignitions due to the ongoing long-term drought.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 1152 AM CDT Sat Jul 25 2026  ·  Valid 251700Z - 261200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
SCATTERED DRY T-STORMSCENTRAL/SOUTHERN MONTANA
CRITICALMUCH OF THE PACIFIC NORTHWEST INTO THE NORTHERN ROCKIES

Meteorological conditions amid numerous wildfires and 90-95th percentile ERCs across the northwestern U.S. will lead to another day of expansive, and potentially dangerous, fire concerns.

A Scattered Dry Thunderstorm risk was introduced across central/southern Montana where a greater coverage of thunderstorms and lightning ignitions are possible atop receptive fuels. Forecast soundings depict a hot and well-mixed environment this afternoon with deep inverted-V profiles, ample MUCAPE, and 25-35 kt LCL-EL winds, indicating thunderstorms will be capable of severe wind-potential and less precipitation efficiency (See the Day 1 Convective outlook). The Isolated Dry Thunderstorm risk area was expanded into northwestern South Dakota and northeastern Wyoming based on the latest observations and forecast guidance. Lightning ignitions are also possible outside of the Isolated Dry Thunderstorm highlights, on the periphery of heavier precipitation cores where fuels are receptive.

Despite moderate RH recoveries across portions of the Pacific Northwest, values are quickly declining and will approach critical thresholds (15% or less) by peak heating. Southwesterly to westerly sustained surface winds are expected to exceed 20 mph, perhaps approaching 25 mph in terrain-favored areas. Locally extremely critical conditions may be possible where these stronger sustained winds overlap critically low RH, in addition to wind gusts of 30-40 mph particularly in the Columbia Basin and western/central Montana. These conditions will further exacerbate the fire environment, impacting any new/ongoing wildfires and control efforts.
