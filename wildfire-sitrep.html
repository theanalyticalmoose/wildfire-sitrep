Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

A hot, dry, and unstable air mass will continue to build across the West. Daytime RH will drop to 3-15% with poor overnight recovery across much of California, the Northwest, Great Basin, northern Rockies, West Slope, and central High Plains. Temperatures are forecast to exceed 100°F in lower elevations of these regions and peaking near 110°F in inland California, the southern Great Basin, and western Arizona. Locally breezy west winds of 12-20 mph (gusts to 35 mph) are expected in the Cascade gaps, northern Sierra Front, northern Great Basin, and northern Rockies. Monsoon moisture remains limited in the Southwest. Isolated to scattered wet thunderstorms will favor the Mogollon Rim, southeast Arizona, and southwest New Mexico, while isolated dry thunderstorms are possible in southeast California, the southern Great Basin, and the southern Sierra. In the East, showers and thunderstorms will briefly diminish in Florida but will continue along the Gulf Coast into the Lower Mississippi Valley. Temperatures will remain near normal east of the Mississippi River, except in parts of New Hampshire, Vermont and Maine, where persistent dry coastal conditions will keep temperatures about 15°F above normal. It will be quite warm and dry over Alaska’s eastern Interior, particularly near the Al-Can border, with increasing potential for new ignitions from isolated lightning. Hot, dry, and breezy conditions will result in moderate potential across Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 0218 AM CDT Fri Aug 07 2026  ·  Valid 071200Z - 081200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

Modest westerly mid-level flow will be found across the region today. As diurnal heating contributes to deep vertical mixing, surface winds around 15 mph with stronger gusts are likely. Additionally, the deep mixing will contribute to relative humidity falling to perhaps as low as 5% across favored areas across the region, with minimum afternoon relative humidity between 10-15% elsewhere. Given very receptive fuels across the area, elevated to locally critical fire weather conditions are likely.

Thunderstorms are expected along the western periphery of a mid-level ridge where the advancing monsoonal moisture will be found. The northern edge of the monsoonal moisture will likely be constrained to the mid and upper levels initially, leaving a very dry sub-cloud layer in place. High-based thunderstorms that do develop here will likely feature significant evaporation of falling precipitation leading to little, if any, appreciable rainfall. Additionally, evaporative cooling beneath the cloud layer will result in gusty/erratic thunderstorm outflows.

Localized fire weather concerns will be possible across portions of California, particularly the interior portions of Southern California. Here, hot temperatures into the 110Fs will contribute to relative humidity into the single digits. However, relatively calm winds should limit the overall elevated fire weather conditions to a more localized nature.
