Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

The highest confidence for isolated dry thunderstorms today covers north central and eastern Idaho, extending into southern Montana and western Wyoming. These storms will become wetter as they move eastward. A lower probability, but potentially high-impact, area for isolated dry thunderstorms extends from eastern Washington to northwest Montana. A break in the monsoon begins today as drier air moves into central and western Arizona, shifting the thunderstorm focus into New Mexico. Even drier conditions will expand across the Southwest by tomorrow. Meanwhile, isolated to scattered mixed wet/dry thunderstorms will continue for Colorado and the Front Range. Very low RH of 3-15% is expanding across the Mojave Desert and much of the Great Basin, with poor overnight recovery of 10-30% in many locations. Unseasonably low RH of 8-20% will also develop in the Southwest, West Slope, Inland Northwest, and northern Rockies. Temperatures will be 5-15°F above normal across many of these areas, particularly the Southwest, though outside of thunderstorm activity winds will remain relatively light except for the Cascade gaps. Widespread heavy thunderstorm rains are possible across parts of the Dakotas and the Missouri River Valley, spreading eastward tonight. Lingering showers and a few thunderstorms will continue across New England, while locally heavy rainfall focuses along and south of a stationary frontal boundary from central Florida to the Lower Mississippi Valley. South Florida is expected to remain hot and dry with a high risk for significant fire potential.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 1245 AM CDT Thu Jul 30 2026  ·  Valid 301200Z - 311200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
No Risk Areas Forecast. The outlook delineates no Critical or Elevated risk areas.

Isolated to perhaps scattered thunderstorms are forecast from central/eastern Idaho into western Montana during the afternoon and evening. An IsoDryT area has been maintained as probabilities of receiving more than 0.1" of rain is less than 50%. Southern portions of the IsoDryT area were trimmed due to some pockets of recent wetting rain. There remains some forecast uncertainty regarding coverage and location of storms due to the smoke that could inhibit insolation and thus terrain circulations that would aid convective development. Additionally, a cluster of high-based storms may persist of overnight in portions western/central Montana.

Locally elevated conditions are likely again across portions of the Inland Northwest into the western/northern Great Basin. West-southwest sustained winds of 10-15 mph with gusts of 20-25 mph amid minimum RH of 5-20% are expected across these areas.
