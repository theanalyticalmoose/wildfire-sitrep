Fire Weather

Predictive Services Discussion

Widespread critical to extreme conditions will develop across the Inland Northwest by late morning. These conditions are expected to worsen into the afternoon and evening while spreading eastward into the Northern Rockies and the far northern Great Basin. Unseasonably strong wind gusts of 35–50 mph are anticipated in many areas, with minimum RH 5-25%. Localized gusts of 60+ mph are likely over portions of northwest Montana tonight. Under these extreme conditions, many existing fires will display extreme growth, and new ignitions will be extremely resistant to control. Ahead of the strong cold front, a very large expanse of record or near-record heat will affect areas from inland California and the Southwest to the Great Basin and Northern Rockies, with some locations approaching monthly records for August. RH of 3–15% will return across inland California, the Great Basin, southwest Wyoming, and the West Slope. Moderate southwest winds may create elevated conditions in these areas for several hours. Pronounced drying is also expected in northern Arizona and the Four Corners, with minimum RH falling to 7–18%. Very isolated dry thunderstorms are possible for southwest Montana and north central Idaho into the Rockies with wet thunderstorms for central and eastern New Mexico. Cooler- than-average temperatures east of the Mississippi River Valley with areas of strong to severe thunderstorms from the Ohio River Valley to the Southeast.

SPC Day 1 Fire Weather Outlook

Issued 0216 AM CDT Sat Aug 01 2026  ·  Valid 011200Z - 021200Z  ·  Mirrored live from spc.noaa.gov
EXTREMELY CRITICALPORTIONS OF THE COLUMBIA BASIN IN CENTRAL/SOUTHEASTERN WASHINGTON AND NORTHERN OREGON
CRITICALEASTERN OREGON AND FAR WESTERN IDAHO INTO EASTERN WASHINGTON
EXTREMELY CRITICALAND NORTHWESTERN/NORTH-CENTRAL MONTANA

Upper-level ridging will remain in place from the Desert Southwest into the northern High Plains today as a potent upper-level trough moves onshore across the Pacific Northwest and British Columbia. Simultaneously, a surface low will amplify across southern Alberta as a dry cold front progresses rapidly east-southeastward through the Pacific Northwest and northern Rockies. This setup will foster widespread elevated to critical fire weather concerns across an expansive area of the eastern Pacific Northwest, northern Rockies, and northern Great Basin, with extremely critical conditions forecast for portions of the Columbia Basin in central/southeastern Washington and northern Oregon.

ERCs are commonly approaching or exceeding the 95th percentile, and a few large wildfires are already ongoing across this region. This combination of meteorological and fuel conditions will support extremely critical fire weather conditions (with winds potentially exceeding 25+ mph for up to 6 hours in some locations) across portions of the Columbia Basin in central/southeastern Washington and northern Oregon this afternoon and evening. While eventual RH increases are expected later tonight, a longer duration of elevated wind conditions (15+ mph) is also forecast, potentially lasting 12-14 hours for some locations. Only a minor expansion of the northwestern edge of the extremely critical area was made with this outlook cycle.

A broader area of elevated to critical fire weather conditions is forecast farther south from northeastern California and northern Nevada into much of central/eastern Oregon and western Idaho. Across this region, sustained surface winds of 15-25 mph are expected to overlap very low RH of 5-20% and ERCs above the 90th to 98th percentiles.

During the afternoon, southwesterly surface winds of 10-15 mph and RH values of 10-15% will also promote elevated fire weather concerns across much of central Montana. While RH will increase to 15-25% overnight, a stronger northwesterly surge (15-25 mph sustained with gusts to 30-40+ mph) will accompany the passage of the cold front during the overnight hours.
